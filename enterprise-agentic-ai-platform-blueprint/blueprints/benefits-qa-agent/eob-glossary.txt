EXPLANATION OF BENEFITS (EOB) — GLOSSARY AND GUIDE
PPO Plan 2026

This document helps member service representatives explain EOB terminology to members.

===========================================================================
WHAT IS AN EOB?
===========================================================================
An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is a statement sent by the health plan after
a claim is processed. It is NOT a bill. It shows how a claim was adjudicated —
what was billed, what was allowed, what the plan paid, and what the member may owe.

Members typically receive an EOB within 30 days of a claim being processed.
EOBs are available on the member portal and mailed for claims over $50.

===========================================================================
EOB FIELD DEFINITIONS
===========================================================================

AMOUNT BILLED (or "Billed Charges"):
  The full amount the provider charged for the service. This is the starting
  point for adjudication but is rarely what anyone actually pays.

NEGOTIATED DISCOUNT (or "Plan Discount" / "Contractual Adjustment"):
  The reduction applied when a provider is in-network. In-network providers
  have agreed to accept the plan's allowed amount as payment in full.
  Members are NOT responsible for this amount.

ALLOWED AMOUNT (or "Eligible Expense" / "Recognized Charge"):
  The maximum amount the plan will consider for reimbursement for a given service.
  For in-network providers: set by the contracted fee schedule.
  For out-of-network providers: typically based on the Usual, Customary, and
  Reasonable (UCR) rate. The member may be billed the difference between
  the provider's charge and the allowed amount (balance billing).

APPLIED TO DEDUCTIBLE:
  The portion of the allowed amount credited toward the member's deductible.
  Once the deductible is met, this column will show $0.00 for remaining claims.

APPLIED TO OUT-OF-POCKET:
  The total amount counting toward the member's annual out-of-pocket maximum
  (deductible + copays + coinsurance combined).

COPAY:
  A fixed dollar amount the member pays per service (e.g., $30 for a primary
  care visit). Copays typically apply at the time of service and count toward
  the out-of-pocket maximum.

COINSURANCE:
  The percentage of the allowed amount the member pays after the deductible
  has been met. For example, 20% coinsurance on a $1,000 allowed amount = $200.

PLAN PAID:
  The amount the health plan paid directly to the provider (or to the member
  for reimbursement claims). This is after applying any deductible, copay,
  and coinsurance.

MEMBER RESPONSIBILITY (or "Your Share" / "Amount You May Owe"):
  The total amount the member owes — the sum of any deductible applied,
  copay, and/or coinsurance. This is what the provider may bill the member.
  For in-network providers, members cannot be billed above this amount.

NOT COVERED / DENIED:
  Some line items may show as not covered. Common reasons:
  - Service excluded from the benefit plan
  - Prior authorization not obtained
  - Service not medically necessary per plan criteria
  - Provider is out-of-network and the service required in-network
  - Claim submitted after the filing deadline
  - Duplicate claim submission

REMARK CODES / REASON CODES:
  Alphanumeric codes explaining how each line item was processed. Common codes:
  - CO-45: Charge exceeds fee schedule/maximum allowable (contractual write-off)
  - PR-1:  Deductible amount (member responsibility)
  - PR-2:  Coinsurance amount (member responsibility)
  - PR-3:  Co-payment amount (member responsibility)
  - OA-23: Service not covered by plan
  - CO-97: Payment included in allowance for another service

===========================================================================
COMMON MEMBER QUESTIONS ABOUT EOBS
===========================================================================

Q: "My EOB shows I owe $400, but I already paid my copay at the office."
A: The copay is usually applied first. The remaining $400 may be coinsurance
   or deductible being applied to the same visit. Compare the "Member Responsibility"
   to what was paid at the time of service. If the provider is billing more than
   the EOB shows, that may be a billing error — advise the member to contact
   the provider's billing department.

Q: "Why does my EOB show a 'not covered' amount?"
A: This typically means the service was excluded under the plan, or a required
   prior authorization was not obtained. Pull up the specific reason code on
   the EOB for the correct explanation. Do not make coverage determinations —
   if the member disputes the denial, direct them to the appeals process.

Q: "I haven't met my deductible. Why did the plan pay anything?"
A: Some services — like preventive care and copay-based visits — are paid
   regardless of deductible status. Copay-based services (like a $30 PCP visit)
   are not subject to the deductible.

Q: "Why is the 'amount billed' higher than what I have to pay?"
A: In-network providers agree to accept the plan's allowed amount. The
   difference between their billed charge and the allowed amount is a
   contractual write-off — neither the member nor the plan pays it.

Q: "I went to an in-network provider but got a bill from an out-of-network anesthesiologist."
A: Surprise billing protections (No Surprises Act) apply in most cases for
   emergency care and for scheduled procedures at in-network facilities where
   the member could not reasonably select the provider. For disputes, the
   member should contact member services and may be eligible for the
   independent dispute resolution process.

===========================================================================
WHEN TO ESCALATE
===========================================================================
Member service representatives should escalate to Claims or Appeals when:
  - Member is disputing a denial (route to first-level appeals)
  - Member believes a claim was processed incorrectly (route to Claims Review)
  - Member received balance billing from an in-network provider
  - Member is requesting a re-adjudication of a claim
  - The issue involves a coordination of benefits dispute with another insurer

Do not reprocess claims or make coverage determinations from the member services line.
